# Presentations and evidence

Open **Review and share** from a dashboard. Choose **Dashboard**, **Story** or **Infographic**. Expand the preview to inspect the report and export the selected format as a PNG once every chart has queried and rendered successfully.

- **Dashboard** retains the authored canvas layout.
- **Story** places existing sections, commentary and charts in reading order, with room for supporting evidence.
- **Infographic** uses compact headline groups and supporting visuals. Choose the charts that answer the business question in **Choose evidence for the infographic**. It starts with all charts; a saved explicit selection stays selected on reopen. The report states how many charts are included and lists omitted charts. Existing commentary remains in reading order. Choosing evidence does not generate or verify a business conclusion.

These are presentations of the current tab. Changing format preserves metric definitions, chart types, current filters, filter exceptions and drill context. It does not apply or save a new dashboard layout. The report records returned bucket dates and applied access-rule identifiers where supplied by the query. Calendar reporting dates describe the model's clock, not warehouse freshness.

KPI cards have one comparison choice, shared by the inspector and card options: **none**, **vs prior**, **vs last year**, or **vs first shown**. Cards with a reporting grain default to the server's prior-period comparison, including older time-series cards saved as `stat`. Prior/year comparisons use the returned server baseline; a missing baseline stays unavailable instead of falling back to an adjacent row. First shown compares with the earliest returned period and names that date in drafted observations. It requires two distinct shown periods and never skips a missing first value. None displays “comparison off” and drafts no change statement. The headline remains the latest metric value in every mode. Percentage-formatted metrics express their change in percentage points.

Changing the comparison also changes the copied context and review fingerprint, even if query rows remain identical. Dashboard, Story, Infographic and their PNG exports retain the chosen baseline. Existing documents with `spark.compare: first` retain that choice; new UI edits clear competing settings. First shown describes the returned reporting window, not necessarily the start of business history. Existing limitations and returned-row warnings still apply.

**Copy review context** also works while charts are loading or failing. The text reports ready/failed/checking counts, each chart's error or pending state, metric IDs, filters and drill context, returned bucket dates and reported access-rule identifiers. Missing metadata is labeled as unreported; a failed check is not described as a clean data-quality result. Missing or empty infographic selections remain explicit. The copy timestamp identifies the handoff, not warehouse freshness. PNG export still requires every included chart to query and render successfully and a valid, nonempty evidence selection. After recovery, copy again to capture the current checks.

Write the **business question**, **takeaway** and **next action or question**. They appear in the preview, PNG and copied context as authored interpretation. No task or notification is sent by writing a next step. Review these notes against the current numbers and filters. **Mark brief reviewed** records your acknowledgement of the included charts and current brief. Save the dashboard to persist that marker. When returned values, query scope, metric model, evidence selection, display semantics or authored brief change, **Review needed** appears beside the takeaway and stays in the PNG and copied context. Older briefs without a marker are labeled unreviewed. Missing evidence cannot be marked reviewed, and a failed change check never reports a match.

Matching evidence means no change was detected against that recorded review; it does not validate the conclusion or prove the warehouse is current. Refresh queries when checking new data. Review status compares returned results, not background warehouse changes. It stores a SHA-256 fingerprint and review timestamp, not a copy of query rows, and it is not an audit signature or an authorization control. The semantic model is checked conservatively, so a model change can require another review even when the displayed values stay the same. Query timing and cache hits do not trigger a changed-evidence notice. Changing presentation alone preserves a match when the included evidence is the same; selecting a different subset requires review against that subset. A copied tab's remapped chart references require its own review.

Export remains available with an unreviewed or changed-brief notice once the charts are ready. The author can revise the prose and mark it reviewed again; the app never silently rewrites or reapproves the interpretation.

The brief and infographic evidence selection belong to the current tab. **Save dashboard** persists them with the composition. Returning to the dashboard keeps edits in the working document and marks it unsaved; a failed save preserves them for retry. Read-only users can prepare a preview but cannot save changes. Current exploratory filters and drill state remain viewing context, rather than saved filter defaults.

Dashboard and Story retain every chart in the current tab, regardless of the infographic selection. A copied tab receives its own copy of the brief with remapped chart references. If a selected chart is removed or no longer belongs to the tab, the infographic explains the missing selection and blocks export until the author updates it. An explicitly empty selection also blocks export. **Include all charts** restores automatic inclusion of all current charts, including future additions.

## Interpreting the evidence

Open **Build your brief from the evidence** to inspect calculated observations from the included charts. Supported views describe an aggregate value, the latest time bucket and its available calendar comparison, or the highest returned category value (including ties). Rates use percentage-point changes. The calculation uses each published metric's identity and units; it never adds rates or snapshot balances across rows. Missing latest values remain missing, and an unavailable requested comparison does not fall back to a different row.

Each observation exposes its metric definition and declared unit. If a unit is absent, the existing naming-based display inference is explicitly disclosed and travels with the appended text. Declared units take precedence over naming. The six playground metrics declare their units in the bundled model. Values are rounded for reading, while comparisons use the original values and small nonzero changes remain visible.

Default percentage labels and drafted observations preserve which side of 100% a value occupies. For example, 99.96% is not rounded into 100%; precision increases near that boundary, and values closer than six percentage decimals use `<100%` or `>100%`. An exact 100% remains exact. Explicit chart decimal settings are respected; raw values and comparison calculations are unchanged.

**Add observation to takeaway** appends the statement, source chart, metric, reporting basis, current filters, access-rule identifiers where supplied, and data caveats. **Add next question** appends a data-quality check or a follow-up supported by the model's reachable dimensions and query validation. Existing prose is preserved, and the author can edit the result before saving. These are local calculations and suggestions, not a live AI-provider call or proof of causation.

Limited results, malformed/repeated dates, multiple simultaneous breakdowns and charts displaying transformed comparison values explain why they cannot produce a single-series draft in this version. Other supported charts remain available. Calculated prose is a starting point for review, not an automatic explanation of why the business changed.

Missing values, gaps, invalid results and truncation are observations about returned queries, not proof of a broken warehouse. Findings explain the evidence, its impact on interpretation, and suggested checks. With one time dimension, each full breakdown combination is checked separately for internal gaps and repeated periods. Another segment's rows cannot fill its gaps, and different observed start/end dates do not establish missing data. Limited results do not support gap inference. Examples name the affected breakdown values; counts cover all affected segments, with up to three examples. In Story and Infographic, each affected chart shows the finding and its evidence beside a numbered reference. Full impact and suggested checks appear in a full-width Data notes block below the section and remain visible in PNG exports. Identical findings in the same reporting scope share one note that names every affected chart; different filters, dates, access rules or evidence remain separate. The live chart evidence dialog, Dashboard presentation and copied context retain their detailed findings. A known excluded partial period is labeled **Reporting scope** and is not counted as a data-quality issue. Its exclusion remains visible in the report.

Presentation tables show up to 20 returned rows and explicitly state whether the table is an excerpt. CSV export from the live chart contains its returned rows, which may themselves be query-limited. The PNG includes static column headings and drill labels, while interactive chart controls are omitted.

Rendering checks establish that the charts can be displayed. They do not certify source completeness, metric ownership, business conclusions or causal explanations. Inspect a live chart's information control for metric definitions, filters and query evidence.

## Local acceptance evidence

`e2e/presentation-journey.spec.ts` exercises the messy starter, an intercepted AI proposal, real proposal application, story arrangement, real sample queries, evidence selection, PNG download and brief save/reopen. It compares query payloads and KPI values across all three formats and verifies that selection leaves the underlying charts unchanged. Further scenarios check drilled scope, filter exceptions, missing values and gaps at a narrow viewport, failed-save recovery, tab isolation and unavailable/empty evidence. The provider response is mocked; this is not evidence of live AI output quality.

These product views are separate from the Gateway site's staged walkthrough, watch mode and editorial overview. Testing the local product does not verify that separately hosted demo or production deployment.
